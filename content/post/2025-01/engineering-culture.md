> We are what we repeatedly do. Excellence, then, is not an act, but a habit.
>
> _- Aristotle_

A team's culture is defined by its shared values and consistent practice of
those values by every individual. Consistently achieving our goals as a team
requires strong collaboration. A shared set of values, we all believe in clearly
indicates whats important to us as a team and make collaboration towards
achieving common goals that much easier. These values are non-negotiable, being
part of this team means subscribing to these values and incorporating them into
our work ethic. As a team we will derive our engineering habits and processes,
based on these values.

## Our Values

![Values](/images/2025-01/engineering-culture/dilbert-values.png)

... that said, shared values do provide a strong framework for decision making
and allow us to transcend our individual beliefs to work together as a strong
team.

### Respect

Respect is consistently recognizing the value an individual provides. Respect
towards every member of your organization is key to collaboration and trust.
This is our building block towards creating an engineering culture where open
dialogue is possible and foster innovation.

### Quality

Quality is consistently beating expectations of your customers. Every edge of
your work should reflect your passion for the product and delight your
customers. For an engineer, customer is anyone who directly or indirectly uses
your product. This can be fellow engineers using your libraries or consulting
you for your expertise, testing teams that proxy for real customers, marketing,
support and services teams that manage our customer relations and most
importantly the paying customer.

### Shared Ownership

Ownership is a sense of pride in your product. While no single individual owns a
piece of code or the product, every member of the team owns the product.
Ownership is demonstrated by stepping up and constantly improving your product
even when you are not responsible. A simple act of filing bugs or improvements,
documenting a common task shows ownership.

### Aligned Autonomy

Aligned autonomy is the key to scale our organization while innovating. As long
as we are aligned in our vision and team mission, we can achieve the right
balance between our tactical and strategic goals. Innovation thrives with
autonomy and if autonomous individuals/teams can align their actions to the
team's mission we achieve excellence.

![Aligned Autonomy](/images/2025-01/engineering-culture/autonomy.png)

Put simply the stronger alignment we have, more autonomous we can grant
ourselves.

### Simplify

True genius lies in removing complexity while achieving the required
functionality. Keeping things simple is a continuos process, we accumulate
complexity as we add features and grow our business. In the infrastructure and
networking domain, everyone provides the features and functionality, what sets
us apart from our competition is how simple we can make operating complex
networks. Simplicity in our solution mean productivity for our customers.

### Automation

A process that is not automated is just a guideline. Automation is not just an
efficiency tool, it forces us to be honest with our workflow and optimizes our
operational burden. Automation allows us to measure accurately and tune our
processes to our needs.

### Bias for Action

Balancing the need for perfection with the reality of business is critical to
success. Make a decision with the facts we have at hand, review and revise when
necessary. Being aware of risk is sometimes enough to mitigate it at a later
date.

![Bias for Action](/images/2025-01/engineering-culture/dilbert-action.png)

### Learn and Teach

Learning and continuous improvement is imperative in modern engineering
organizations. Periodic technical presentations and a forum to present
engineering achievements creates opportunity for engineers to take pride in
their work and others to learn outside their scope of work.
